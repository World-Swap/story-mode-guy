Viewers forgive soft focus and a shaky pan, but they leave when they have to strain to understand someone. If your video looks fine and sounds hollow, hissy or echoey, you are probably losing people in the first few seconds without anyone telling you why.

When you search for how to fix bad audio in video, the answer is almost never a plugin. Nearly every problem comes down to three things: the mic is too far from the mouth, the room is bouncing your voice back, or the levels are wrong. Here is how to deal with each one at the source, before you ever open an editor.

Who it's for

Creators whose picture is fine but the sound lets them down. People will forgive a soft-focus shot, a shaky handheld pan, even a slightly blown-out sky. They will not forgive sound they have to strain to understand. The instant a video sounds hollow, hissy, or echoey, the brain reads it as "amateur" and the thumb reaches for the next thing. This guide teaches you to record clean, clear, professional sound with whatever you already own.

Why does my voice sound echoey and distant on camera?

Two reasons. First, distance: each time you double the gap between mic and mouth, the voice drops to about a quarter of its strength, so the camera raises gain to compensate and amplifies the air conditioner and room along with it. Second, built-in mics are usually omnidirectional, so they hear the room as well as you. A directional mic such as a cardioid or shotgun helps, but it is not a zoom lens. Even a shotgun sounds best a foot or two from the mouth.

Then deal with the room. Hard, parallel surfaces make your voice arrive at the mic several times. Do the clap test: clap once where you record, and if you hear a ring or slap, treat it. Hang blankets, add a rug or curtains, pick the bedroom over the kitchen, and get closer to the mic, which shifts the balance toward direct voice and away from reverb for free.

What is the best microphone placement for interviews and vlogs?

Match the mic to the job. For seated interviews, a shotgun on a boom pole just out of frame, angled down at the mouth from 6 to 18 inches, sounds cinematic, and a lav clipped six to eight inches below the chin is the alternative. For seated talking-head work, a USB or lav mic sits 6 to 12 inches away, just below the frame and slightly off-axis so plosives on p and b pass across the capsule instead of into it. For vlogs and run-and-gun, a wireless lav clipped high on the chest at 6 to 8 inches is hard to beat.

Place lavs on a lapel or collar edge where fabric will not brush them, loop the cable into a small P shape and tape it behind the clip as strain relief. Let the camera record a scratch track too, as a sync reference and a backup if the wireless drops.

What audio levels should I record and export at?

Record so that peaks land between minus 12 and minus 6 dBFS, with your average voice around minus 18 to minus 12. That leaves at least 6 dB of headroom for a laugh or an emphasized word. Set gain from a real test line at your true speaking volume, not a timid test, test. Turn off auto-gain, because it pumps the noise floor up and down. Clipping at 0 dBFS slices the peaks flat and cannot be repaired, so when in doubt record a little quieter.

Always monitor with headphones plugged into the recording device. Meters show level, but only your ears catch rustle, dropouts, buzz and the AC kicking on. For delivery, aim near minus 14 LUFS with true peaks at minus 1 dBTP or lower. In the edit, run noise reduction first, then EQ with a cut below about 80 to 100 Hz, then compression, a de-esser if needed, and normalization last.

Common questions

What is the best microphone for video?

It depends on how you shoot. A lavalier suits interviews and anyone who moves, a shotgun suits scenes and off-camera dialogue, and a cardioid USB mic suits seated voiceover and tutorials. For most creators, a wireless lav kit is the highest-impact purchase because it keeps a mic near the speaker's mouth.

Can I fix bad audio in editing?

Partly. Noise reduction, EQ, compression and de-essing improve clean recordings, and a light touch on noise reduction avoids a watery, robotic voice. But editing cannot undo clipping, heavy reverb or a voice buried in noise. Those are recording problems, so treat post as the last ten percent.

How loud should background music be under dialogue?

Roughly 15 to 20 dB below the voice, quiet enough that you notice it only if it stops. Use ducking so the music drops automatically while someone speaks and rises in the gaps. Check the mix on earbuds and a phone speaker, where music masks speech most easily.

What is room tone and why record it?

Room tone is two seconds or so of silence from the actual location, recorded with everyone quiet. Noise-reduction tools use it as a sample of what to remove, and editors use it to patch gaps between cuts so the background does not drop to dead silence.
