Most bad footage is not a talent problem. It is a forgotten setting: the wrong shutter speed for the frame rate, audio peaking into the red, or a timeline that does not match the clip. When you are on location with the light fading and a client watching, you do not want to reason it out from scratch. You want a card in your bag.

The point is a three-second check before you hit record, not memorizing everything.

What settings should a videography cheat sheet cover?

The cards that earn their place are the ones tied to mistakes you cannot fix afterward. Frame rate and shutter come first: shutter is roughly double the frame rate, so 1/50 at 24 or 25fps, 1/60 at 30fps, 1/120 at 60fps. Slow-motion math belongs there too, since 120fps played on a 24fps timeline runs five times slower. Next are resolution, codec, and bitrate, including why 10-bit matters for grading and why your card's sustained write speed must match your bitrate.

Add a picture profile card covering Standard, Flat, and Log, with the reminder to expose Log slightly bright and respect its base ISO. An exposure card should list ND strengths: ND8 is 3 stops, ND16 is 4, ND64 is 6, ND1000 is 10. Zebras at about 70 percent help hold skin tones. Audio, lighting, movement, and shot-list cards finish the set.

What audio levels and checks should I follow on a shoot?

Viewers forgive rough footage but switch off bad audio fast. Set gain so normal speech averages around minus 12 dBFS and the loudest peaks stay below minus 6. Zero dBFS is clipping, and clipped audio cannot be repaired. Recording around minus 20 means you will raise noise when you boost it later. Distance matters more than price: a modest mic 30cm from the mouth beats an expensive one two meters away.

Use a checklist rather than memory: headphones plugged in, meters actually moving, a windscreen outdoors, fresh batteries in mics and transmitters, room tone recorded at each location, and noisy fridges or A/C turned off. For delivery, loudness targets are a separate job. A reference card would list minus 14 LUFS for YouTube and Instagram or TikTok, minus 16 for spoken-word and podcasts, and minus 23 for broadcast under EBU R128.

How do I use quick-reference cards on location?

Treat them as a pre-roll ritual. Before you hit record, run a three-second check: frame rate matches the project, shutter is double the frame rate, and audio meters are moving. Keep printed cards laminated in the camera bag, with a PDF copy on your phone for when you are unsure. Pick one frame rate per project, say 24 for cinematic or 30 for web, and only leave it for deliberate slow-mo clips.

The closing cards are a pack-and-shoot checklist and export recipes. Check batteries for camera, mic, monitor, and gimbal, formatted cards, an ND filter, a white balance set to the dominant light, and a printed shot list. For exports, match frame rate end to end, use H.264 MP4 for YouTube and vertical 1080x1920 at 10 to 15 Mbps for Instagram or TikTok, and keep a higher-quality master for clients.

Common questions

What shutter speed should I use for 24fps video?

Use about 1/48s, which most cameras round to 1/50s. The rule is to set shutter to roughly double your frame rate, the 180-degree shutter angle. That gives natural motion blur. For 30fps use 1/60, and for 60fps use 1/120.

How do I calculate slow motion?

Divide the capture frame rate by the playback rate. Shooting 120fps on a 24fps timeline gives 5x slower, 240fps to 24fps gives 10x, and 60fps to 30fps gives 2x. Keep the timeline at your project frame rate and note that high frame rates need more light.

Which ND filter do I need for sunny days?

ND8 (3 stops) suits overcast or golden hour, ND16 (4 stops) bright but not harsh daylight, and ND64 (6 stops) full sun. ND1000 (10 stops) is for very bright sun at wide apertures. A quality variable ND covers most daylight work with one filter.

What export settings work for YouTube?

Export H.264 MP4 at your project frame rate. For 1080p use about 16 Mbps, and for 4K about 45 Mbps, with AAC audio at 320 kbps. YouTube re-compresses everything, so exporting at decent quality preserves detail. Mix loudness near minus 14 LUFS.
