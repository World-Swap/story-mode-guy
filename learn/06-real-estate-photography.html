If you want to learn real estate photography, or you just need your Airbnb listing to stop looking dark and cramped, the good news is that interior work is mostly method, not talent. Rooms do not move, so you can lock your settings, put the camera on a tripod, and repeat the same steps in every room.

A buyer scrolling a listing app decides in a couple of seconds whether to tap, and a guest booking a stay does the same thing. Bright, straight, well-exposed rooms with a real view out the window are what get the click, and they are also a skill agents and hosts will pay for. Here is how the pros actually get that look.

Who it's for

Aspiring side-hustlers and hosts shooting their own listings. A buyer scrolling a listing app decides in under two seconds whether to tap or keep scrolling. A guest booking an Airbnb does the same. That decision is made almost entirely on the first photo — which means the person who can make rooms look bright, straight, and sellable holds real leverage. This guide teaches you that craft from a standing start, using gear you may already own.

What camera settings should I use for interior real estate photos?

Interiors are the easy part of the technical side. Put the camera on a tripod, set ISO 100 (or your base ISO), pick an aperture around f/8, and shoot in RAW. At f/7.1 to f/9 you get the whole room sharp from the near wall to the far one. Do not go past f/11, because diffraction starts softening the frame without adding any useful depth of field in a room-sized space.

Let the shutter be whatever it needs to be. On a tripod, a slow shutter is a feature, since it gathers window and lamp light at a clean ISO. Use Aperture Priority at f/8 and let the camera choose, then fire with the 2-second timer or a remote so your finger does not add vibration. Switch off in-body or lens stabilization once the camera is locked down, because it can hunt for movement that is not there and add a faint blur.

How do I stop windows from blowing out in room photos?

A camera cannot hold the bright view outside and the shadowed room inside in a single exposure the way your eyes do. A white, featureless window is the biggest giveaway of an amateur interior shot. There are three ways around it. The beginner method is to expose for the room, push it a touch brighter than correct, and pull the window highlights back in editing. That works well when windows are a small part of the frame.

The pro method is flash and ambient blending. Expose one frame for the window, then take a second identical frame with a hotshoe flash bounced off the ceiling, and blend them. The third option is HDR bracketing: 3 to 5 frames about 2 stops apart, changing only shutter speed and never aperture. Whatever you choose, aim for realism. If a viewer can tell you blended the frames, you overdid it.

Where should I stand and how high should the camera be for room photos?

Shoot from roughly chest height, about 48 to 55 inches off the floor. Higher shrinks the room and shows too much floor, and lower exaggerates the foreground. Then stand in or near a corner and aim across the room so two walls are visible. Pointing straight at one flat wall makes a room look shallow, while the two-wall angle shows depth and how the space flows.

Keep the camera perfectly level so door frames and corners stay vertical, and use your digital level on every frame. Stay around 18 to 22mm full-frame equivalent for most rooms. Going ultra-wide stretches the edges and makes rooms look dishonestly large. Before you shoot, walk the property, open all blinds, turn on every light, close toilet lids, hide cords, and check mirrors so you and the tripod are not in the reflection.

Common questions

Do I need a professional camera to shoot real estate photos?

No. A crop-sensor camera with a 10-18mm lens and a sturdy tripod is a workable starter kit, and a modern phone with an ultra-wide lens can handle entry-level Airbnb work. What matters most is a wide lens, a tripod, and a consistent method. Phones struggle more with straight verticals and low-light files.

What is the best lens focal length for interior photography?

On full frame, about 16 to 24mm, or 10 to 16mm on a crop-sensor body. Most rooms look best around 18 to 22mm equivalent. Save the widest end for tight spaces like bathrooms, since ultra-wide angles bow straight lines and make rooms look unrealistically large.

How many photos should a real estate listing have?

A typical listing needs about 20 to 35 strong photos, not 200 mediocre ones. Pick the best frame of each angle, cull fast, edit one hero shot, sync those adjustments across the set, then fine-tune. Consistent white balance and brightness make the whole listing look cohesive.

What is a twilight exterior and why do agents pay extra for it?

A twilight shot is taken in the 20 to 30 minutes after sunset, when the sky is deep blue and the home's lights glow warmly against it. Use a tripod, f/8, ISO 100, a long shutter, and bracket every few minutes. It looks aspirational, which is why it is a premium add-on.
