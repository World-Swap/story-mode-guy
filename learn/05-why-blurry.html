Why Are My Photos Blurry?

When photos come out blurry, the first instinct is to blame the camera or the lens. In most cases the cause is one of three things: the camera moved, the subject moved, or the lens focused on the wrong spot. Each leaves a different fingerprint in the image, which means you can usually tell which one it was by looking at the photo at 100%.

Once you can name the type of blur, the fix is specific. Below is the quick diagnosis, the shutter speed rules for handholding, and what to do when the subject itself is moving. It applies to DSLRs, mirrorless cameras, and phones.

Who it's for

Frustrated hobbyists whose shots keep coming out soft. You spent real money on a camera, and somehow your phone still takes sharper pictures. It is the single most demoralizing thing in early photography — and it is almost always caused by one of just three things. This guide teaches you to name the blur, fix its cause, and never guess again.

How to tell camera shake, subject motion, and missed focus apart

Zoom in to 100% and ask three questions. Is anything in the frame perfectly sharp? If yes, your camera was still and focus worked, so the culprit is subject motion: the background is crisp and the moving thing is streaked. If nothing is sharp, is the whole frame evenly soft or smeared in one direction? That is camera shake. If something is sharp but it is the wrong thing, like the ear instead of the eye or the fence behind the face, you missed focus.

The fastest tell is bright pinpoints such as catchlights or streetlights. A sharp image keeps them as dots. Camera shake stretches every one into a short line. Subject motion streaks only the moving object's highlights while the background keeps its dots. A uniformly hazy, low-contrast image with nothing crisp points to a dirty lens, diffraction from an extremely small aperture, or poor glass.

What shutter speed stops camera shake when handholding

The reciprocal rule says your shutter speed should be at least 1 divided by your focal length: 1/50s at 50mm, 1/200s at 200mm. On a crop-sensor body, multiply the focal length by the crop factor first (1.5x for most brands, 1.6x for Canon), so a 50mm lens behaves like 75mm and wants about 1/80s. Round up when unsure. People breathe and sway, so for portraits use at least 1/125s.

When the light is too low for a fast shutter, work through three options in order. Open the aperture, since a cheap f/1.8 prime is one of the best anti-blur upgrades. Raise ISO, because a grainy sharp photo beats a clean blurry one. Add support by bracing on a wall or planting your elbows. Tuck your elbows in, cradle the lens from underneath, and squeeze the shutter at the bottom of a slow exhale. Turn stabilization off when on a tripod.

How to photograph moving kids, pets, and sports without blur

Subject motion is cured only by a faster shutter. Starting points: 1/250s for walking kids, 1/500s for playing pets, 1/1000s for running and sports, and 1/2000s for birds and cars. Motion across the frame needs a faster shutter than motion coming toward you, so a car passing left to right smears much more than one driving at you.

A reliable setup is Shutter Priority (S or Tv) with the freezing speed set, Auto ISO with a ceiling such as 6400 so noise cannot run away, continuous autofocus (AF-C or AI Servo) to track the subject, and burst mode so you can pick the peak frame. If the light will not allow a fast enough shutter, panning is a good fallback. Use a slow shutter around 1/30s and swing the camera with the subject, which keeps it sharp while the background turns into speed lines.

Common questions

Why are my photos blurry even though my camera is expensive?

Gear is rarely the cause. The usual reasons are a shutter speed too slow for your focal length or subject, focus landing on the wrong spot, or a dirty lens. Zoom to 100% and check whether anything is sharp. That tells you which problem to fix.

How do I know if blur is from camera shake or a moving subject?

If the background is sharp but the subject is streaked, the subject moved. If the entire frame is soft, and bright highlights have turned into short lines, the camera moved. A faster shutter fixes both, but how fast depends on the cause.

What is the minimum shutter speed for handheld photos?

Use 1 over your focal length as a baseline, so 1/50s at 50mm, and multiply the focal length by the crop factor on crop-sensor cameras. For portraits of people who may sway or blink, go to 1/125s or faster. Image stabilization helps with shake but not subject motion.

Is it better to raise ISO or accept a slower shutter in low light?

Raise ISO. Some noise at a high setting such as ISO 3200 is usually usable, while motion blur cannot be fixed afterward. Open the aperture first if you can, then raise ISO, and use Auto ISO with a ceiling so the camera keeps your shutter speed.
