If you own a modern camera drone, real estate drone video is one of the few paid niches where the barrier is skill rather than budget. The flying itself is the easy part. What you are really selling is legal compliance, consistent smooth moves, a shot list that covers a property completely, and a tight edit.

Who it's for

Side-hustlers, agents, and hosts filming their own listings. A single smooth aerial reveal of a house sells the whole listing before a viewer reads one word of the description. That thirty-second clip is worth real money to the agent, the host, or the seller who needs it — and if you own a modern drone and learn a handful of repeatable moves, you can be the person who shoots it. This guide turns your drone into a paid tool for filming property tours that actually look professional.

Do I need a license to shoot real estate with a drone?

In most countries, flying for a business purpose, including a listing or a rental promotion, requires a commercial remote pilot certification. In the US that is the FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate, earned by passing a knowledge test. Other places have their own paths: the UK CAA, Transport Canada, EASA countries, and Australia's CASA each set their own registration and credential rules. Treat any list like this as a starting map, not legal advice, and confirm the current requirement with your aviation authority before your first paid flight.

Beyond certification, check airspace at every property, because homes can sit within a few miles of airports, heliports, or hospitals. Where authorization is needed, get it before flying and stay under its ceiling. Carry liability insurance, since some clients ask for proof. Never fly over people who are not part of the shoot or over moving traffic, and keep the drone in visual line of sight.

What shots should I include in a property drone video?

Work from a repeatable exterior shot list so no property goes uncovered. Start with the establishing shot, high and pulled back, showing the home in its setting. Add the front-of-home hero, a clean straight-on or gently angled view of the facade, and the approach, a slow forward push down the driveway toward the front door. Then cover the lot boundaries, the backyard and outdoor living areas like pool, deck, and patio, a high-angle pass of the roofline and condition, and a tasteful neighborhood context shot showing nearby parks or water.

Keep the composition centered on the client's property. Sweeping wide to show the whole street films other people's homes, which raises privacy issues, so avoid hovering over or pointing the camera into a neighbor's yard, pool, or windows. Practice the full list on your own home or a friend's with permission, then cut a 30-second tour. You will quickly learn which shots you are missing.

How do I film a cinematic house reveal and orbit?

The rise-and-reveal is the most reliable crowd-pleaser. Start low behind a hedge, tree, or fence with the gimbal tilted up, then rise straight up while slowly tilting the camera down, so the house appears over the obstacle and fills the frame. Two alternatives are the pull-back reveal, starting tight on the front door and flying backward and up, and the foreground wipe, where a tree or roofline passes close through the frame before the house appears.

For the orbit, use your drone's automated Point of Interest mode if it has one. Set a wide enough radius that the drone stays well away from the roofline, trees, and wires on the far side, and rehearse the circle first. Pick the slowest speed that fits your battery window, and position so the sun rakes across the house rather than blowing out one side. Beginners fly too fast, so cut your speed roughly in half and use gradual starts and stops. Golden-hour light helps a lot.

Common questions

Can I fly a drone for a real estate listing as a hobbyist?

Generally no. A flight that supports a listing or rental promotion is commercial, and most countries require commercial certification for it. In the US that means FAA Part 107. Verify the current rules with the FAA or your national aviation authority before any paid or business-related flight.

What camera settings work for property aerial video?

A reliable daylight starting point is 4K at 30fps, shutter at 1/60s, ISO 100, and white balance locked to a sunny preset. Lock exposure so brightness does not jump over dark treelines, and use a normal color profile if you are not confident grading flat footage.

Do I need ND filters for real estate drone video?

They are the highest-impact accessory after the drone. In daylight, holding a 1/60s shutter lets in too much light, so an ND filter cuts it and keeps motion smooth. A small kit of ND8, ND16, and ND32 covers most conditions, with ND16 a good all-day-sun default.

What is the best time of day to film a property with a drone?

The golden hours: the first hour after sunrise and the last hour before sunset. They give warm, directional light that flatters most homes. Midday sun is harsh, flattens the scene, and casts hard shadows. Position your orbit so the light rakes across the house.
