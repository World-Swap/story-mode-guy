Most people who stall at the timeline are not short on talent. They are trying to trim, pick music, fix color and rewrite the story all at once. If you have searched for how to edit a video and still do not know where to start, the answer is not a new editor, it is an order of operations.

The workflow below is the same in Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve and CapCut: set up, cull, assemble, tighten, pace, mix, add text, correct color, export. Here are three of the stages where beginners lose the most time, explained so you can use them on your next project today.

Who it's for

Anyone who films but stalls at the timeline. You have the footage. It is sitting on a card, or already dumped into a folder called "New Folder (3)", and every time you open your editor you feel the same thing: paralysis. Where do you even begin? This guide fixes that by giving you an order of operations — the exact same sequence, every project, forever — so editing stops being a wall of choices and becomes a checklist you walk down.

How do I set up a video editing project so it does not lag or lose files?

Build the folders before you import anything. A simple structure of 01_FOOTAGE, 02_AUDIO, 03_MUSIC, 04_GRAPHICS, 05_PROJECT and 06_EXPORTS prevents the dreaded media offline message, because editors store links to your files, not the files themselves. Copy footage off the card to your computer, then copy it again to a second drive or the cloud before you format anything.

Next, match the timeline to the footage. If you shot 4K at 24fps, make a 3840 by 2160, 24fps sequence. The easy way is to drag your first clip onto the new-item area in Premiere, or onto an empty timeline in Resolve. If playback stutters, you probably do not need a faster computer. Generate proxies, which are small stand-ins that play smoothly while the full-quality files wait for export, and start them the moment import finishes so they are ready by the time you finish culling.

How do I make my cuts feel smooth and professional?

Cut on action. Place the edit in the middle of a movement, such as a reach for a door or a turn of the head, and the motion carries the viewer across the cut so it almost disappears. Then use J-cuts and L-cuts so sound and picture do not change at the same instant. In a J-cut the next clip's audio starts before its picture, and in an L-cut the current audio continues over the next shot, which is the backbone of interview b-roll. To build one, unlink or separately trim the audio and video edit points.

Also trim every clip's head and tail. Cut the wind-up, the trailing pause and the um. Use ripple delete so gaps close automatically. Vary shot length on purpose: quick cuts build energy and a held shot afterward lands with weight. If you are unsure whether to keep a shot, cut it. Edits almost always improve by being shorter.

What export settings should I use for YouTube and Instagram?

Start from your editor's preset, since presets get you most of the way, then check three things: resolution, codec and bitrate. Export at the same frame rate as your timeline, because upping 24 to 60 on export cannot add smoothness that was never shot. Use H.264 in an MP4 for nearly everything, with AAC audio at 48 kHz.

The guide's starting recipes are roughly 12 to 16 Mbps for YouTube 1080p, 35 to 50 Mbps for YouTube 4K, and 8 to 12 Mbps for vertical Instagram or TikTok at 1080 by 1920. Client deliveries can run 40 to 80 Mbps, or ProRes. Aim for about minus 14 LUFS loudness so platforms do not turn you down. Finally, watch the entire export in a normal media player before uploading. Missing audio, a dropped title or the wrong aspect ratio only show up there.

Common questions

What order should I edit a video in?

Set up and import, cull the keepers, build an assembly edit in story order, tighten to a fine cut, work on pacing, mix music and sound, add titles and captions, do a light color correction, and export. Skipping ahead, like polishing color on an unlocked cut, is the main reason edits balloon.

What is an assembly edit?

It is the first pass on the timeline, where your only job is getting the story in the right order. It will be long and rough, and that is correct. For talking content, write a paper edit first and check that the audio alone tells the story, like a radio show, before you lay b-roll over it.

What audio levels should I mix to?

As starting points, keep dialogue peaks around minus 6 to minus 12 dB, background music around minus 18 to minus 24 dB, and never exceed 0 dB. Target roughly minus 14 LUFS overall. Duck the music under speech with your editor's auto-duck or keyframes so words always cut through.

Do I need to learn Premiere, Resolve and CapCut separately?

No. The workflow is software-agnostic, so once you know what to do at each stage you can switch editors in an afternoon. Resolve has a capable free version, CapCut is quick for social and captions, and Premiere is a standard in many studios. Pick one and learn it well.
