Product Photos That Sell

Shoot-at-Home Photography for Etsy & Small Shops

If your product photos look dull next to the competition on Etsy, Shopify, or Amazon, the problem is almost never your camera. A shopper cannot pick up your item, so a small image on a phone screen is doing all the selling, and it gets a couple of seconds to look trustworthy.

You can fix this at home with a window, a piece of white foam board, and a tripod, for roughly $30 in total. Here is what to set up, how to light a product so it looks expensive, and how to shoot the clean white-background image that marketplaces expect.

Who it's for

Etsy, Shopify & Amazon sellers photographing their own products. A shopper will never hold your product, smell it, feel its weight, or turn it over in their hands. They will look at a photo on a screen the size of a playing card and decide, in about two seconds, whether you are worth their money. That photo is not "part of" your listing — it is your storefront, your salesperson, and your first impression, all at once. This guide teaches you to shoot photos that do that job, at home, with the gear you already own.

How to set up a home product photography studio for about $30

You need four things: a window with bright, indirect daylight, a white bounce card (foam board or poster board), a sweep of white paper curving from table to wall so there is no horizon line, and a cheap tripod. Put a table beside the window, with the window to the side of the product, not behind the camera. A tripod lets you use low ISO and shoot every item from the same height and angle.

Skip ring lights, which leave a donut reflection on shiny items, and cheap LED panels, which often carry a green or magenta cast. Novelty backdrops look cheap on camera. A collapsible lightbox is worth considering only if you shoot many small items like jewelry and want fast, consistent catalog shots. It will not help with styled lifestyle photos, and anything bigger than a shoebox is better shot at the window.

How to light products so they look expensive

The larger and softer the light source relative to the product, the better it looks. A window a few feet from a small item is enormous by comparison, which is why it works. Place it 45 to 90 degrees to one side to reveal texture and shape, then put the white card on the opposite side to bounce light into the shadows. Slide the card closer for brighter fill, farther for more drama.

If direct sun is hitting the product, hang a white sheer curtain, tracing paper, or a thin sheet over the window. Then turn off every room light. Mixing daylight with a yellow bulb gives the product two color temperatures that cannot be corrected together. Do not chase a shadowless look either. A gentle gradient from bright side to softer side is what makes an object look three-dimensional and premium.

The camera settings and white-background method for sharp, accurate products

For a camera on a tripod, use Aperture Priority around f/8 at base ISO (100 on most bodies). Go to f/11 for deeper items, but avoid f/16 and beyond, where diffraction softens the frame. Use the 2-second self-timer so pressing the shutter does not shake anything, and shoot RAW so you can fix color later. On a phone, turn off Portrait mode, lock focus and exposure, use the main 1x lens, and move the phone rather than pinch-zooming.

For the white background, remember that cameras try to average a scene to gray, so a white backdrop comes out dull. Add roughly +1 to +1.7 stops of exposure compensation while watching that the product itself does not blow out. Amazon's main image requirement is pure white (RGB 255, 255, 255) with the product filling about 85% of the frame, so confirm that in editing. Include a gray or white card in your first frame to set accurate color.

Common questions

Do I need a professional camera to photograph products for Etsy?

No. A modern smartphone works well when it is paired with good light and a tripod. Light and setup account for most of the quality. Use the main lens, turn off Portrait mode, lock focus and exposure, and use a timer to avoid shake.

What is the best light for product photography at home?

A large window with bright, indirect daylight, positioned to one side of the product, with a white card bouncing light onto the shadow side. Diffuse direct sun with a sheer curtain and switch off all room lights to avoid mixed color temperatures.

What aperture should I use for product photos?

Around f/8 for most items, because it keeps the whole product sharp without much diffraction softening. Use f/11 for deeper or very small items. Wide apertures like f/1.8 blur parts of the product, which looks like a focus mistake on a listing.

How do I get a pure white background for Amazon?

Overexpose slightly using exposure compensation, about +1 to +1.7 stops, so the white backdrop does not render gray. Protect detail on the product, then clean up in editing until the background reads RGB 255, 255, 255. Amazon also expects the product to fill about 85% of the frame.
