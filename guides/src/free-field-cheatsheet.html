CARD 01Exposure Quick-Start

Start here, then fine-tune. These are reliable starting points for common scenes — not rigid rules.

Bright daylight portraitSTARTING POINT
Aperture f/2.8Shutter 1/500sISO 100

Wide aperture for a soft background. Watch for blown highlights on skin.

Landscape, everything sharpSTARTING POINT
Aperture f/8–f/11Shutter autoISO 100

Tripod if the shutter drops below 1/60s. Focus about a third into the scene.

Indoors / low lightSTARTING POINT
Aperture f/1.8–f/2.8Shutter 1/125sISO 800–3200

Let ISO rise before the shutter drops too slow to hand-hold. Noise beats blur.

Remember

Aperture controls depth. Shutter controls motion. ISO is the last resort for brightness. Move one, compensate with another.

CARD 02The 10-Second Sharpness Fix

Blurry shots aren't bad luck — they're one of three problems. Diagnose, then fix.

What you seeThe causeThe fix
The whole frame is softCamera shakeFaster shutter (≥ 1/focal length); brace or use a tripod
Background sharp, subject smearedSubject motionFaster shutter to match: walking 1/250s, running 1/500s+
Something's sharp — just not your subjectMissed focusSingle-point AF on the eye; stop down to f/2.8–f/4
⚠️The silent culprit

Most blur is a too-slow shutter you never noticed because the camera was on Auto in dim light. Watch that number.

CARD 03The Light: Golden & Blue Hour

The single biggest upgrade to any photo is when you shoot it.

  • Golden hour — the hour after sunrise / before sunset. Soft, warm, directional. The most flattering light there is.
  • Blue hour — the ~20 minutes after sunset. Deep blue sky, glowing lights, glassy water. Bring a tripod.
  • Stay late. The best color often comes after the sun is gone. Most people leave too early.
  • Face away from the sun too. The eastern sky often glows a soft pink while everyone points west.

CARD 04Video: The One Rule That Fixes the Look

If your video looks like a home movie, it's usually the shutter speed. Match it to your frame rate — the 180° rule.

Frame rateShutter speedUse it for
24 fps1/50sThe cinematic, "film" look
30 fps1/60sStandard web / social video
60 fps1/125sSmooth motion & slow-motion
🛠️Bright day?

To keep that slow shutter outdoors you'll need an ND filter — think of it as sunglasses for your lens. It's the one accessory that makes video look pro.

CARD 05Drone: Pre-Flight in 30 Seconds

Most crashes and flyaways are prevented on the ground. Run this before every flight.

  • Firmware & app updated; propellers checked for cracks
  • Batteries charged (drone and controller); SD card in with space
  • Enough GPS satellites & home point set
  • Return-to-Home altitude set above the tallest nearby obstacle
  • Wind & weather within your drone's limit
  • Airspace checked and legal to fly; clear line of sight
⚠️Two habits that save drones

Land at ~30% battery — don't chase one more shot. And in the US, any flight for money needs an FAA Part 107 certificate. Always verify current rules before you fly.
