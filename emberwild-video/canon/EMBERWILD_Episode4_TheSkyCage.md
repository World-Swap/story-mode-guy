# EMBERWILD

### Episode 4 — "The Sky Cage"

**Full comic book script · 22 pages**
Original story. Art style: anime-influenced linework, expressive eyes, flowing hair, Art Nouveau curves. **Full-colour interiors.** **Cover accent: storm-violet (#6d6f9c) — the Sky Clan's issue.**

> **This script replaces the void draft titled "The Sky Clan."** That draft was written before Episode 3 existed and contradicted it. Everything below is built on Ep3's canon: the Rings, the First, the cords, the Pact, and Ep3's closing hook — *"You're too late for the mountain. The Ironfolk already have the one who remembers the sky."*

---

## SERIES BIBLE (quick reference for the artist)

Continues **directly** from Episode 3. Rook stood watch on the moor; a hooded stranger walked out of the dark and told him the mountain was already lost.

**Returning cast:** ROOK (young chief, the stolen bone knife, bonded to CINDER) · SORREL (13, bonded to MOTH) · BRAM (17, bonded to THORNCLAW) · KEEPER YARROW (ancient, blind one eye) · SEDGE (marsh, bonded to the serpent RILL) · MARSH-MOTHER BRACKEN (matriarch, pale serpent) · **WREN (19, unbonded, keeper of the knot-cords)**.

**New this issue:**
- **SHRIKE** — Sky Clan, thirties, hooded, one eye. **His storm-bird VESPER is dead** — killed in a failed rescue that also took his eye. A Vael with no other half left, and nothing to bond to again. He has been walking the Ironfolk's straight line because he has nothing else to walk. *He is what an Ironfolk is made from, and everyone can see it but him.*
- **KESTREL** — leader of the Sky Clan, bonded to the storm-bird **SQUALL**. Proud, and currently hollowed out: she already tried to take the cage and lost eleven people. She will not try again.
- **KITE** — thirteen, Sky Clan, bonded to the storm-bird **TERN**. Sharp-mouthed, flies before she thinks. *The mirror of Wren: bonded at thirteen, and cannot stop talking about it, in front of a woman who waited nineteen years for nothing.*
- **SKY-KEEPER HALCYON** — ancient. The Sky Clan's keeper of the ring-lore. **The one who remembers the sky.** Currently caged.
- **THE CAGE** — an Ironfolk holding-structure driven into the mountainside. All straight lines on a peak that has none. It **hunts bonds**: anything with a half is seen coming.
- **ASH** — an Ironfolk cutter. Appears on the final page only. Speaks one word.

**Visual note:** the Sky Clan's country is thin air, violet dusk, and vertigo. The Vael curve-language goes *vertical* here — wind-curves, feather-curves, updraughts. The Cage is the only straight thing on the mountain and it should hurt to look at.

**Tone:** dread and altitude. A refusal that isn't cowardice. A rescue that only works because of a wound. Then the thesis of the whole saga, said out loud on page twenty-one.

## PAGE ONE

*(Splash page — 1 panel)*

**Panel 1.** Full-page splash. Dawn over the peaks — storm-violet shading to cold rose. Enormous, indifferent mountains. And running up the flank of the nearest one, impossibly, a **dead-straight scar**: a cut road climbing where no road should climb, ending in a hard geometric shape lodged near the summit like a splinter in skin. Far below, tiny, the gathered party on the ridge trail. At their head, apart from them: **SHRIKE**, hooded.

CAPTION: They had come to the mountain to ask for help.

CAPTION: The mountain had already been asked.

TITLE (lower third): **EMBERWILD** — Episode Four: *The Sky Cage*

## PAGE TWO

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Morning, the ridge trail. The party strung out. Shrike walks ahead of them all, not with them.

CAPTION: He had told them his name and nothing else for half a day.

**Panel 2.** Rook catches up to him. Cinder pads between them, wary.

ROOK: You said we were too late. Too late for what, exactly.

SHRIKE: For the polite version.

**Panel 3.** Shrike points, without slowing, at the straight scar on the mountain.

SHRIKE: They cut that in eleven days. Straight up a face that's killed better climbers than you. They don't switchback. They don't rest. They put a door in my mountain and they took our Keeper through it.

**Panel 4.** Rook, absorbing it.

ROOK: Halcyon.

SHRIKE *(a beat — he wasn't expecting the name)*: ...You know her name.

ROOK: A marsh-woman told me the gathering isn't about numbers. It's about finding whoever remembers the sky. Yours is the only tribe that looks up for a living.

## PAGE THREE

*(5 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Shrike stops. Turns. First good look at him: hood back now — a hard, weathered face, one eye gone, the socket seamed with old scarring.

SHRIKE: Then you're late by nineteen days. Ask the next question.

**Panel 2.** Rook doesn't. Yarrow does — she's come up the trail behind, blind eye toward Shrike like she's reading him.

YARROW: Where is your bird, Sky-man?

*(Beat. Let it sit.)*

**Panel 3.** Shrike, absolutely flat. This is a wound with all the feeling scraped off it.

SHRIKE: Her name was Vesper. She's on that mountain. So's my eye.

**Panel 4.** The party has gone very quiet. Sedge and Bracken exchange a look — the marsh understands slow bonds and dead ones better than anyone.

SHRIKE: Don't. I've had the face you're making done at me by experts.

**Panel 5.** Close on Yarrow, and this is the important beat of the page — she isn't pitying him. She's *frightened* of him, and hiding it badly.

YARROW *(quiet, to Rook, as Shrike walks on)*: A Vael with no other half left. Nothing to bond to.

YARROW: Boy. That is the exact recipe. Watch him.

## PAGE FOUR

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Higher. The air thinning. The party labouring. Sedge is having a terrible time and is not being quiet about it.

SEDGE: Your air is *broken.* There isn't enough of it, and what there is *stabs.*

ROOK: Breathe slower. It gets easier.

SEDGE: It gets easier for the *cat.* Rill was made for water. Not... vertical nonsense.

**Panel 2.** Bram, enjoying this far too much. Thornclaw bounds ahead easily.

BRAM: Some of us like a challenge, marsh-man.

**Panel 3.** Behind them, **Wren** climbs steadily, cords at her waist, saying nothing. Bracken watches her, not the trail.

BRACKEN: You've not complained once.

WREN: I've got nothing on my shoulders but me. Turns out that climbs quite well.

**Panel 4.** Bracken doesn't have an answer for that. Wren didn't say it to wound. That's what makes it land.

## PAGE FIVE

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** A shadow flicks across the rock. Everyone's head snaps up.

**Panel 2.** **KITE** drops out of the sky on **TERN** — a storm-bird, grey-violet, huge — and lands hard on an outcrop above them, spear levelled. Thirteen, furious, showing off.

KITE: Ground-crawlers. You're off your ground. Turn around and crawl back down to it.

**Panel 3.** Rook, hands open. No blade.

ROOK: We came to speak with Kestrel. We're Vael, same as you.

KITE: We're *nothing* the same as you. We have wings. You have... a cat.

**Panel 4.** Sorrel, instantly and completely offended on Cinder's behalf.

SORREL: He's not "a cat." His name is Cinder, and he's been alive longer than you've had that spear. And I've got a lynx *too.* So that's two of us who'll out-stubborn you.

## PAGE SIX

*(5 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Kite, circling on Tern, cocky — and then she sees Shrike, and the cockiness falls straight off her face.

KITE: ...Shrike?

**Panel 2.** Shrike doesn't look up at her.

SHRIKE: Go and tell her. She'll want to hear it from you before she sees me.

**Panel 3.** Kite hesitates — torn, young, out of her depth — then wheels away hard.

**Panel 4.** Sorrel watches the storm-bird go, wistful despite herself. And then — because she is thirteen and honest and cannot help it — she says it to the wrong person.

SORREL: How old were you? When you bonded.

KITE *(calling back over her shoulder, already lifting)*: Thirteen!

SORREL: Me too!

**Panel 5.** And there, one step behind them, is **Wren**. Nineteen. Nothing on her shoulder. Face completely still.

*(No dialogue. Do not cut away quickly. Let the reader do the arithmetic.)*

## PAGE SEVEN

*(1 panel — splash)*

**Panel 1.** Full-page splash. **The Sky Clan's eyrie** — and it is *not* the proud citadel the forest Vael expected. Wind-carved spires, rope bridges, nest-platforms lashed to the rock — and half of it **abandoned**. Empty perches. Bridges cut and hanging. Storm-birds too few for the number of roosts. Tarpaulins over what used to be homes. A people who have shrunk.

And from every high point, the same view: the straight scar, and the Cage on the far peak, close enough to see. They live looking at it.

CAPTION: The forest Vael had expected a fortress full of proud people.

CAPTION: They found forty-one survivors who could not stop looking at the same mountain.

## PAGE EIGHT

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** **KESTREL** on the central platform, **SQUALL** mantling behind her. She is magnificent and she is exhausted. She does not greet Rook. She looks past him.

KESTREL: You brought him back.

**Panel 2.** Shrike, at the edge of the group.

SHRIKE: I brought them. Different verb.

**Panel 3.** Kestrel comes down the steps. Ignores Rook entirely. This is between the two of them and the strangers are furniture.

KESTREL: Eleven, Shrike. I counted them out loud so no one could pretend it was ten.

SHRIKE: I know the number.

KESTREL: You *made* the number.

**Panel 4.** Silence. Shrike takes it. Doesn't argue. Somehow that's worse.

---

## PAGE NINE

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Kestrel finally turns to Rook. Cold, quick, done with it.

KESTREL: Whatever he promised you, no. We don't go back to that mountain. Fly down. Take your cats.

**Panel 2.** Rook — and here he does the thing Episode 3 made him swear to do.

ROOK: I'm not going to argue you into it. I'm going to *ask* you, and if you say no, we leave.

**Panel 3.** Kestrel blinks. That was not the shape she was braced for.

KESTREL: ...You'll leave.

ROOK: I swore on a dead woman's cord that no one takes anyone. Ever again. That includes taking your people to a fight they've already lost.

**Panel 4.** Bracken steps up beside him. Marsh and forest, shoulder to shoulder — and Kestrel clocks it, because that shouldn't be possible.

BRACKEN: He's telling the truth, sky-woman. I made him swear it. I watched him do it. It cost him something.

BRACKEN: Ask him what's on his belt sometime.

## PAGE TEN

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Kestrel, wrong-footed, goes on the attack the way tired leaders do.

KESTREL: Then ask and be refused. What do you even want her *for?* You want a Keeper to make your fighters brave? You want wings to carry your fire down a valley?

**Panel 2.** Rook: quiet, and completely unshakeable. This is the mission statement of the entire saga, said plainly.

ROOK: I don't want your wings.

ROOK: I want what's in her head.

**Panel 3.** Kestrel stares.

ROOK: There's a broken circle of stones on the moor where the bonds still hold. My people took the one who remembered the sky, and it broke the sky, and now every soul in the world comes in halves that don't arrive.

ROOK: The gathering isn't about numbers. It's about finding whoever remembers the sky, and putting the memory back together. Your Keeper is a piece of it.

**Panel 4.** Beat. Kestrel's face does something complicated.

KESTREL *(low)*: ...Say that first part again. The stones where the bonds hold.

---

## PAGE ELEVEN

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Kestrel walks to the platform's edge. Stares at the Cage on the far peak. Something has just clicked into place for her, and she hates it.

KESTREL: I thought they took Halcyon to shut her up. To take the lore out of the world.

**Panel 2.** She turns back. And there's fear in her now, of a different quality.

KESTREL: But you're telling me there's a place that *mends bonds*, and they went for that too.

KESTREL: They're not silencing anything. They're **collecting.**

**Panel 3.** Wide — the whole party absorbing it. Yarrow closes her good eye. Sedge swears under his breath. Wren's hand goes, unconsciously, to the cords at her belt.

**Panel 4.** Close on Rook. The horror of it arriving.

ROOK: ...They're doing what my ancestors did.

ROOK: They're taking the one who remembers.

---

## PAGE TWELVE

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Kestrel spreads a scratched slate — a map of the Cage, drawn by someone who paid for the information.

KESTREL: Fine. You want it, here's why you can't have it. This is what killed eleven of mine.

**Panel 2.** The Cage, diagrammed: a straight box driven into rock, one approach, and — ringing it — a series of iron posts.

KESTREL: The posts. We thought they were fence. They're not fence.

**Panel 3.** Kestrel, flat.

KESTREL: They *feel* us. Not our feet — our **bonds.** The moment a bonded Vael crosses that line, every mask on that mountain turns around at once. Squall and I got within forty paces. Forty.

**Panel 4.** Shrike, from the back. First time he's volunteered anything.

SHRIKE: Vesper crossed it. That's when they turned.

SHRIKE: She was the alarm. She was always going to be the alarm. We just didn't know we were carrying one.

## PAGE THIRTEEN

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** The party, stuck. Bram voices the obvious.

BRAM: So we go unbonded. Send someone who leaves their lynx behind.

**Panel 2.** Yarrow, gently, because he's young.

YARROW: Child, the bond isn't a coat. Moth could be a valley away and Sorrel would still light that fence like a bonfire. It's not *proximity.* It's the *having.*

**Panel 3.** Silence. And it lands on the group slowly, one face at a time — because the answer has been walking behind them all week, carrying the records, saying nothing.

*(Art: hold on the group. Then a slow rack of focus past them all — to Wren, at the back, who has already worked it out and is standing very, very still.)*

**Panel 4.** Wren. Quiet. Not brave yet. Just accurate.

WREN: It's the *having.*

WREN: ...Well. There's one thing I've never had.

## PAGE FOURTEEN

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Bracken, instantly, violently:

BRACKEN: **No.**

**Panel 2.** Wren, turning on her — nineteen years of this, all at once, and she keeps her voice perfectly level, which is worse than shouting.

WREN: Marsh-Mother. Every winter of my life, someone has looked at me the way you're looking at me now. Poor Wren. Nineteen and empty. Wren the *unfinished.*

WREN: I have been the saddest thing in that village since I could walk.

**Panel 3.** Close, two-shot. Bracken and the girl she raised.

WREN: And there is a fence on that mountain that only lets through people with nothing in them.

WREN: For the first time in my entire life the thing that's wrong with me is *the answer.* Don't you dare take that off me too.

**Panel 4.** Bracken — the woman who ordered this girl to correct her out loud, and got exactly the person she asked for. Her face breaks and she doesn't let it.

BRACKEN *(barely)*: ...I raised you to correct me.

WREN: You did. You were very good at it. I'm correcting you.

## PAGE FIFTEEN

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Kestrel, watching this, quietly re-evaluating everything.

KESTREL: Your unbonded girl walks into an Ironfolk cage alone.

WREN: My unbonded girl has a name, and she's the only person on this mountain that fence can't see.

**Panel 2.** Kestrel and Rook, eye to eye. The ask, again, properly.

KESTREL: What do you want from the Sky Clan, forest-chief. Say it as a question or don't say it.

ROOK: Will you fly the noise? Everything you have, on the far side, loud as you can — so every mask on that mountain turns *away* from the one person walking in?

ROOK: You'd be the alarm on purpose.

**Panel 3.** Long beat. Kestrel looks at the Cage. At Shrike. At Kite, thirteen, watching her.

KESTREL: ...Eleven of mine are on that rock because I let Shrike ask me a question I wanted to say yes to.

KESTREL: Ask me again.

ROOK: Will you help us get her back.

**Panel 4.** Kestrel. Squall mantles behind her, huge.

KESTREL: **Yes.**

## PAGE SIXTEEN

*(3 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Dusk. The launch. Storm-birds pouring off the eyrie into violet air — the whole Sky Clan, forty-one strong, banking hard toward the far peak. It is beautiful and it is a decoy and everyone knows it.

CAPTION: Forty-one of them went up to be seen.

**Panel 2.** Below, in the rocks: Wren, alone, cords left behind in Sorrel's hands. Rook, Bracken and Sorrel at the last cover.

BRACKEN: The cords stay here. If it goes wrong—

WREN: —they burn before they're touched. I know. I swore it on the moor. I'll swear it again if it makes you feel better.

BRACKEN: It doesn't.

**Panel 3.** Sorrel, small, holding the cords, and Moth pressing against Wren's leg — the cub, again, choosing her.

SORREL: Moth's doing it again. Looking at you.

WREN *(not looking back — she can't afford to)*: Tell her I'll be back to be looked at.

## PAGE SEVENTEEN

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** The line of iron posts. Wren steps over it.

Nothing happens.

**Panel 2.** Nothing continues to happen. She stands in the dead ground between the posts and the Cage — the first Vael to ever stand there — and the silence is the loneliest thing in the book.

CAPTION: Nineteen years of nothing, and it had finally been useful.

CAPTION: She had never felt it more.

**Panel 3.** Above and away: the sky *screaming.* Storm-birds and light and every soot-mask on the mountain turned the wrong way.

**Panel 4.** Wren walks into the Cage. Straight lines, cold iron, right angles — everything her world isn't.

## PAGE EIGHTEEN

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Inside. And it's *not* a dungeon. It's worse: it's a **workroom.** Clean. Ordered. A chair. Instruments. Rolls of straight-ruled records. They have been doing this carefully and for a long time.

**Panel 2.** And in the chair — **SKY-KEEPER HALCYON.** Ancient, tiny, unbroken. Not chained. She doesn't need to be. She looks up without surprise.

HALCYON: You're not one of them.

WREN: No.

HALCYON: You're not one of *mine* either.

WREN: No. I'm the marsh. I'm the one who holds the knots.

**Panel 3.** Halcyon's eyes change. Two record-keepers, recognising each other across a wound that's older than both of them.

HALCYON: ...Knots. You kept *knots.* All this time, and you kept knots.

HALCYON: We sang. They cut. You *tied.* Three tribes, three ways to hold the same thing.

**Panel 4.** Wren, urgent, hand out.

WREN: Keeper, we have to go. Now.

## PAGE NINETEEN

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** And Halcyon doesn't move.

HALCYON: Child. Do you know why I'm in this chair?

**Panel 2.** Halcyon, calm, gesturing at the neat instruments, the ruled records.

HALCYON: They didn't take me to silence me. Nobody built *this* to make an old woman stop talking.

HALCYON: They took me to **ask** me.

**Panel 3.** Close on Wren. The floor going out from under her.

HALCYON: Every day. Politely. Endlessly. *How did the sky break, Keeper. Tell us how the sky broke.*

HALCYON: They want the same thing you want, girl. Exactly the same thing. They just don't say please.

**Panel 4.** Wren — and this is the moment the Pact stops being a nice line and becomes load-bearing. She takes her hand *back*. She straightens. And she does the one thing that separates her people from the people who built this room.

WREN: ...Then I'll say it.

WREN: Sky-Keeper Halcyon. **Will you come with us. Please.**

## PAGE TWENTY

*(3 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Halcyon looks at the girl for a long moment. Something very old and very tired sits up in her.

HALCYON: Nineteen days in this chair, and you're the first one to ask a question you'd accept "no" to.

HALCYON *(rising)*: Take me home.

**Panel 2.** Out — fast. Wren half-carrying her down the straight corridor. Behind and above, the sky still shrieking with storm-birds. Alarms that don't sound like alarms: a rhythmic iron *tock, tock, tock.*

**Panel 3.** The line of posts ahead. Wren gets Halcyon over it —

— and the posts **light up.**

*(Halcyon is Sky Clan. Halcyon is bonded. Wren was never the alarm. Her passenger was.)*

## PAGE TWENTY-ONE

*(4 panels)*

**Panel 1.** Chaos on the rocks. Masks pouring downhill in straight lines. Kestrel and Squall hammering down out of the dark. Rook, Bram, Sedge and the lynxes hitting the flank. It's a scramble, not a battle — they are running, and it is working.

**Panel 2.** Shrike, and this is his page: standing in the open on the straight road, unbonded, invisible to the fence, hauling Halcyon up behind Kite onto Tern.

SHRIKE: *Go.* Go, go —

KITE: Shrike — come *on* —

SHRIKE *(and he almost doesn't; he looks up the mountain toward where Vesper is, for one beat too long)*

**Panel 3.** Then he turns his back on it and runs. **He chooses the people.** Yarrow, watching from the rocks, lets out a breath she's been holding since page three.

**Panel 4.** Safe ground. Halcyon down, Kestrel gripping her Keeper's hands, forty-one people alive. And Rook, standing apart, hearing what Wren tells him — and it is the thesis of the entire saga, delivered exhausted, on a cold rock, by a girl with nothing on her shoulder.

WREN: Rook. They're asking her how the sky broke.

WREN: They want what we want. They're doing what your people did to the First. Taking the one who remembers.

ROOK: ...Then what's left? What's the difference between us and them?

WREN: **We asked.**

WREN: That's it. That's the whole difference. It's very small and it's the only one we've got, so don't lose it.

## PAGE TWENTY-TWO

*(3 panels — the hook)*

**Panel 1.** Later. Grey pre-dawn on the retreat trail. The party moving fast and quiet. Bram, at the rear, stops — something in the scree.

**Panel 2.** An **Ironfolk cutter**, alone, cut off from the column. Wounded. Cornered against the rock. Soot-mask, forge-red eye-slit, hands shaking. Bram's spear is already up. Cinder is already low.

CAPTION: They had fought the Ironfolk in the reeds. On the moor. In a burning forest a whole world behind them.

CAPTION: They had never taken one alive.

**Panel 3.** And the mask **speaks.** One word. Small, and human, and impossible.

IRONFOLK: ...*please.*

*(Hold on Rook's face. He is carrying a knife that his people used to take someone who begged. Cut to black.)*

CAPTION: *Next: Episode Five — "The Deserter."*

### END OF EPISODE FOUR

**Page count:** 22

**Threads advanced:** the gathering reaches the Sky Clan · Halcyon recovered — the first surviving piece of the sky-memory · the Ironfolk are revealed to be **collecting rememberers**, not silencing them · Kestrel joins on an *ask*, not a conscription · Wren's unbondedness reframed from wound to instrument.

**Threads planted:** the Ironfolk are hunting *how the sky broke* — the same question the Vael are chasing, by the opposite method · the iron posts that hunt bonds (how do they *feel* a bond? — pays off in Ep5 via Ash) · Shrike as a Vael standing at the fork that makes an Ironfolk · Halcyon's "we sang, they cut, you tied — three tribes, three ways to hold the same thing" (**the fourth way, and who cut, is Act III**) · Moth still choosing Wren.

**Character arcs seated:** **Wren** (pitied → the only one who can walk in → the one who names the saga's thesis) · **Rook** (keeps the Pact when breaking it would be easier, and hears his ancestors' crime being committed by his enemy) · **Kestrel** (hollowed refusal → says yes to a question, not a demand) · **Shrike** (a bondless Vael who chooses people over a dead half — the road *not* taken to becoming Ironfolk) · **Bracken** (raised a girl to correct her, and gets corrected on the thing she cares most about).

**Canon compliance:** Wren is 19, marsh, unbonded, cord-keeper ✔ · the Pact is named and load-bearing twice (p9, p19) ✔ · goal is memory, not manpower (p10) ✔ · the Rings and the First are the argument that moves Kestrel (p10–11) ✔ · Ep3's "what do they want with a place that mends souls?" is answered (p11) ✔ · the Sky Clan youth is **Kite**, not Wren ✔ · Ep5's "never taken one alive / heard one speak" is set up exactly (p22) ✔.
