# EMBERWILD

### Series Bible · v2

An original 13-episode comic saga. Fantasy that opens into something larger.
**Full-colour interiors, full-colour covers.** ~22 pages per issue. A complete, planned story — no endless filler.

> **What changed since v1:** interiors moved from black-and-white to full colour · Episode 3 *"Old Wounds"* was written and drawn, and locks a large amount of new canon (the Rings, the First, the theft, the cords, the pact) · Episode 4 is retitled **"The Sky Cage"** per Ep3's closing hook · Sedge, Bracken, Wren and Ash are locked · the Ironfolk origin theory is now on the page.

---

## 1 · The Pitch

**Logline.** The Vael are a nomadic forest people, each bonded from childhood to a flame-lynx — a horned wildcat whose life is tied to their own. When the advancing Ironfolk burn the last of the old forests, a reluctant young chief named Rook must gather the scattered Vael tribes and uncover the truth of the broken ring of light in the sky, and of where his people really came from.

**The hook.** It begins as earthy tribal fantasy and turns, at its heart, into something stranger and bigger — the quiet mystery of the shattered sky pays off as the origin of an entire people. Warm and sincere up front; epic and unsettling by the end.

**Why it sells as a set.** A finite, pre-planned saga with a real ending. Each issue introduces a new tribe, a new bonded creature, and a new colour identity — so every cover in the run is its own object while the thirteen still read as one shelf.

---

## 2 · The World

**The world is made of curves.** Rivers, roots, hair, horns, the broken ring itself — nothing in the Vael's world runs straight. This is the visual and thematic spine of the series. The one thing that draws a straight line is the enemy.

**The Riven Sky.** A vast shattered ring of pale light arcs overhead, day and night. No living Vael remembers it whole, and no one remembers what broke it. It is beautiful and wrong, and it is the central mystery of the saga.

**The Vael.** Nomadic, tattooed, barefoot; clothed in layered hide, woven bark-cloth, and bone. They move when the land tells them to. Every Vael is born "half a soul" — the other half waits in the fire.

**The bond.** At a fire-ceremony, a bonded creature comes *through* the flame and joins with the child for life. The two are life-linked and can feel each other. The bond cannot be faked: the creatures cannot abide a liar, so only the honest are ever truly chosen. Skill and standing among the Vael are read from the bond itself, never self-claimed.

**The tribes.** The Vael believe themselves the last of their kind. They are wrong. Scattered survivor tribes each bond with a different creature and carry a different colour:
- **Flame-lynx Vael** — the forest people, Rook's tribe. Bonded to horned wildcats.
- **Marsh Vael** — water-dwellers who bond with serpents. Live in a stilt-and-raft village in the reeds.
- **Sky Clan** — peak-dwellers who bond with storm-birds.
- *(Room to seed more — "the stone ones" are named but unmet.)*

**The Ironfolk.** The antithesis of the Vael. They advance in straight lines, cut the world into geometric scars, and bring fire. They bond with nothing.

## 2b · The Old Wound *(locked by Episode 3 — do not contradict)*

**The Rings.** A broken circle of standing stones on the moor, where the Vael were one people — and stopped being one. The broken sky hangs *directly above* the broken circle. Inside the Rings, **the bonds still hold stronger**. It's faded, but it's real: Sorrel feels Moth louder there; Sedge has never felt Rill so clear in nineteen years.

**The First.** The one among them who remembered the sky *before it broke*. **Where the First stood, the bonds held. Every time.** So the First was not only someone they loved — the First was *a place the bonds still worked*. Both at once. That's what made what came next unforgivable.

**The theft.** When the sky broke, the bonds began to fail and the halves stopped arriving — a people with a counted end. The forest Vael, afraid the moor was dying, decided to leave for the trees. **They did not ask. They took the First, in the night, with a blade cut from the marsh's own bone.** That blade is the curved bone knife Rook now carries — his mother's, and hers before that.

**The cost.** Not the First — *the sky*. The marsh grieved the First and lived, but their bonds went slow and hard (Wren is nineteen and still waiting). The forest's come in three nights: a child asks and the fire answers. Bracken's line: **"Your easy bondings are our stolen ones."**

**Both sides were starving.** The cord records hunger. It does not forgive the theft — but the marsh's telling leaves out that the forest was dying too. Wren says so out loud, in front of everyone, because Bracken raised her to correct her.

**Records.** The forest keeps memory in **smoke and song**. The marsh keeps it in **knots** — cords, kept by Wren. *"Same wound, two ways."*

**The Pact** *(sworn at the end of Ep3, on the First's cord — the moral spine of everything after)*:
> **"When we find them — the sky ones, the stone ones, whoever's left — no one takes anyone. Ever again. We ask. Especially when we're dying."**

**The real goal of the gathering.** Not numbers. Not fighters. Bracken: *"It's about finding whoever remembers the sky. Piecing the memory back together."* The First remembered the sky whole — find how the sky broke, and maybe the bonds could be whole again. For everyone.

**The Ironfolk origin theory** *(raised by Rook and Sedge in Ep3, confirmed from the inside by Ash in Ep5)*: the Ironfolk came to the Rings — they *know what the Rings are*. One of them, taller and differently masked, **looked** at the stones the way a Vael looks, then **looked back** as it withdrew. *"A tool doesn't grieve a thing it's leaving."* The theory: the straight-line people were **Vael too** — the ones whose bonds failed hardest, whose halves never came. A Vael with no other half left, nothing to bond to. **So they bonded to iron instead.** To the straight line. To the thing that doesn't need a soul.

**Open question, deliberately unanswered:** *"What do they want with a place that mends souls?"*

## 3 · Themes

Community and belonging. Identity and origin. The difference between being alone and being bonded. Honesty as the price of trust. Reluctant leadership — a chief who didn't ask for it. The cost of survival. **Asking versus taking.** And, at the end, coexistence chosen over conquest.

---

## 4 · Principal Cast

**Rook** — the tribe's reluctant young chief since his mother's death. Sharp-eyed, quiet, carries his mother's curved bone knife — *which is the stolen blade*. Hair worn loose and wind-blown. Bonded to the flame-lynx **Cinder**. Walks last, so he's the final thing between his people and the dark. Won't lie, even to comfort. *"I keep my word. It's the only thing I've got that isn't stolen."*

**Sorrel** — thirteen; the initiate whose bonding opens the saga. Wiry, brave once she stops trying. Bonded to the cub **Moth**. The story's honest heart, Rook's conscience, and **the namer** — she names Moth in Ep1 and Ash in Ep5.

**Cinder** — Rook's flame-lynx. Scarred muzzle, one torn ear, horned; the runt of his litter. Feels danger before Rook does.

**Moth** — Sorrel's flame-lynx cub. Small, silver-and-orange, oversized paws. *Keeps looking at Wren.*

**Bram** — seventeen; headstrong and proud. His father led before Rook's mother. Bonded to the lynx **Thornclaw**. Rival, then reluctant ally.

**Keeper Yarrow** — the tribe's ancient Bond-Keeper. Blind in one eye; reads the fire. The first to speak the old word — *Ironfolk*. Keeps the forest's memory in smoke and song.

**Sedge** *(locked)* — of the Marsh Vael; serpent-bonded to **Rill**. Wary, marsh-dry humour, tests people before trusting them. Living proof the Vael are not the last. Chose Rook against his own tribe's mood; guides the gathering. *"I said you'd die without me. Keeping my word. Loudly resenting it."*

**Marsh-Mother Bracken** *(locked)* — matriarch of the Marsh Vael. Guarded, straight-backed, teal-and-bone robes, a great pale serpent at her feet. Keeper of the old wound. Raised Wren — and **ordered her to correct her, out loud, in front of others**. Reads the water, not the fire. Swears the Pact.

**Wren** *(locked — Ep3)* — **nineteen. Unbonded.** Marsh Vael, raised by Bracken. **Keeper of the knot-cords**, the marsh's entire record. Carries the First's cord. Still waiting for a half that has never come; stood in the centre of the Rings, asked *"...is someone there?"* — the exact words Sorrel used at the bonding fire in Ep1 — and **nothing came**. Took it without flinching: *"No. I wanted to know. Now I know. That's better than the not-knowing."*
> ⚠️ **Wren is NOT Sky Clan and NOT thirteen and NOT bonded.** An early draft of Ep4 got this wrong; that draft is void.

**Kestrel** — proud leader of the Sky Clan of the peaks; bonded to the storm-bird **Squall**. Bends to no one easily.

**Sky-Keeper Halcyon** — the Sky Clan's ancient keeper of the ring-lore. *Candidate for "the one who remembers the sky."*

**Ash** *(locked — Ep5)* — the Ironfolk deserter. Taken as a child, stripped of her name and her bond, made a "cutter." Gaunt, soot-grey, hair shorn to stubble, an angular brand half-filed off her own brow. Bonded to nothing — *and that absence is a wound, not a boast; it was cut out of her.* Reclaims her name when Sorrel refuses to call her a number.

**Iron Warden Vane** — the Ironfolk antagonist. No bonded soul, by choice. Moves — and cuts — in straight lines. Drawn in hard angles and cold greys so he reads as *wrong* against the Vael's curves. Full reveal held for Ep6.

**The stranger on Ep3's last page** — hooded, one visible eye. *"I've been following the straight line too."* Tells Rook: *"You're too late for the mountain. The Ironfolk already have the one who remembers the sky."*
> ❓ **IDENTITY NOT YET LOCKED.** Options: a Sky Clan survivor fleeing the cage (cleanest — preserves Ep5's "we never heard one speak") · Ash arriving two issues early · a member of an unmet fourth tribe.

## 5 · Visual System

**Style.** Expressive, large-eyed faces; flowing hair; Art Nouveau linework and curves throughout. The Vael world is drawn in organic curves; the Ironfolk in rigid straight lines and hard facets — the contrast is a design rule every artist works against.

**Format.** **Full-colour interiors** (changed from v1's black-and-white); full-colour covers. ~22 pages per issue.

**Issue skeleton.** Cover → warm, grounded middle → one turn → a page-22 hook that hands into the next issue. Keep issues 1–8 earthy so the origin turn lands hard.

### Page & lettering spec *(locked in production)*

- Page **1000 × 1500 px**, 30px margin, live frame **940 × 1440 px**.
- Panels: 3px `#14100d` border, 3px radius, 14px gutters.
- **Captions:** Georgia italic, cream `#efe4c6` box, 2px ink border, drop shadow.
- **Balloons:** uppercase, `#fbf7ec`, 2.5px ink border, 18px radius, tailed (bl/br/tl/tr); speaker name in **bloodwarm**.
- **SFX:** heavy italic, tribe-keyed colour, ink drop-shadow.
- Standard layouts: **L1** splash · **L2** 2-panel · **L3** 3-panel · **L4** 4-panel (wide/half/half/wide) · **L5** 5-panel.

### Cover template

Fixed frame, swappable zones, so all thirteen covers stay a matched set:
- **Fixed:** the `EMBERWILD` wordmark lockup (bottom), the issue box (top-left), crop marks, credit line.
- **Swaps:** episode art, tagline (top), issue number, and the accent bar — recoloured to that issue's tribe.

**Locked taglines:** #1 *"to bond is to stop being alone."* · #2 *"we thought we were the last."* · #3 *"two tribes who each thought they were the last. one knife to undo it all."* · #5 *"you got the fire. we got the ash."*

## 6 · Episode Roadmap (13)

**Act I — The Burning**
1. **The Bonding** ✅ *complete — 22pp illustrated* — a child's first bonding; the Ironfolk fire; the tribe flees west, and a serpent-bonded stranger appears across a river.

**Act II — The Gathering**
2. **Strangers in the Reeds** — first contact with the Marsh Vael; Sedge unmasked; the Vael learn they are not the last. Ends: Bracken names the theft — *"they took the first of us."*
3. **Old Wounds** ✅ *complete — 22pp (art in the old style; needs the colour redo)* — the journey to the Rings; the truth of the theft; Wren's failed bonding; the Ironfolk come for the stones; the Pact is sworn.
4. **The Sky Cage** ⚠️ *retitled from "The Sky Clan" — must be rewritten* — the Ironfolk **already hold** the one who remembers the sky. Up to the peaks, too late; the Sky Clan broken or besieged; a rescue, not a recruitment. Kestrel must be **asked**, per the Pact.
5. **The Deserter** ⚠️ *needs revision* — Ash. She **confirms** the Ironfolk-were-Vael truth from the inside rather than revealing it, and answers *how the Ironfolk know what the Rings are*. The Pact is tested on the enemy.
6. **The Straight Scar** — following the geometric cut to its source; the first real look at Iron Warden Vane and the machine he commands.
7. **The Long Winter** — the gathered tribes starve and doubt Rook; Cinder falls gravely ill; his leadership fractures.

**Act III — The Truth**
8. **The Ruins That Shouldn't Be** — deeper into impossible ruins; the origin mystery sharpens.
9. **The Fallen Ring** — the reveal: the ancestors did not rise from the forest but *fell* from the Riven Sky.
10. **The Sleeping Ones** — inside a buried relic-vessel, the tribes find their ancestors in stasis, and learn the broken sky was no accident but the price of survival.

**Act IV — The Reckoning**
11. **Iron and Flame** — the Ironfolk march on the gathered tribes; the shared origin becomes undeniable.
12. **The Waking** — someone must rouse the sleeping ancestors at a terrible cost.
13. **Emberwild** — not conquest but a fragile new coexistence; the Riven Sky explained; a new generation bonds beneath a changed horizon.
