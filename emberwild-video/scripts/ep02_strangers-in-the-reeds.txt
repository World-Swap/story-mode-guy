EMBERWILD — EPISODE TWO: STRANGERS IN THE REEDS
Full comic script, 22 pages. Supplied by the author. Supersedes the PDF extract.
Tagline: "we thought we were the last."   Cover accent: reed-teal #2f8f7a

NOTE ON FORMAT: header specifies black-and-white interiors - Series Bible v1, SUPERSEDED.
Interiors are full colour. Build in colour.

VISUAL NOTE: the marsh is mist, reeds and teal water - a different world from the forest.
Serpents move like the water itself. Keep the Vael-world curves; the marsh curves in
ripples and coils.
TONE: wonder and wariness, one crisis that forces cooperation, then a cold hook.

p1  SPLASH. First light over the unfamiliar valley. Mist low over a wide teal-dark river
    braiding through tall reeds. High on the wooded slope above, tiny and tense, the forest
    Vael camp clings to the treeline, not yet daring to come down.
    CAPTION: They had walked west until the world ran out of names.
    CAPTION: Fire behind them. Ahead - water, and reeds, and something that watched from the
             far bank and did not run.
    CAPTION: The Vael believed themselves the last of their kind.

p2  CAPTION: No one had slept.
    BRAM: It's still down there. I saw the water move an hour ago.
    ROOK: It didn't attack. It watched us and left. That's not a hunter.
    BRAM: A Vael with a snake, Rook. Coiled round its neck like it belonged there. That's not natural.
    ROOK: Neither's a horned cat that walks out of a fire. To them, maybe we're the wrong ones.
    BRAM: You're going down there.
    ROOK: We need water. We need to know what's ahead. And I need to know if we're really alone.

p3  YARROW: The old stories say the Vael scattered, long ago. To water, to stone, to sky.
            I thought they were only stories to put children to sleep.
    YARROW: If one of those stories is standing on that riverbank - then some of the others
            are true too. And not all of them are gentle.
    SORREL: I'm coming.
    ROOK: No.
    SORREL: You said the bond only chooses the honest. If they're Vael, they'll trust honest
            faster than they trust a chief with a knife.
    ROOK: ...Yarrow. Is she right?
    YARROW (dry): The child is usually right. It's very annoying.
    ROOK: Cinder and me. Sorrel and Moth. No blades drawn. Bram - you hold the slope.
    BRAM: If it goes wrong I'm coming down.
    ROOK: I know. That's why I'm telling you not to.

p4  CAPTION: The marsh swallowed sound. Their own footsteps went quiet.
    SORREL (whisper): Rook. The water. Things are in it.
    ROOK: I see them. Keep your hands where they can see yours. Same as meeting anyone.
    ROOK (thought): There.
    (Ahead, half-seen in mist and reeds: a figure. Still. Watching. A long sinuous coil at its shoulders.)

p5  REVEAL - SEDGE, knee-deep in water among the reeds. Lean, wet-slick hair, marsh-tribe
    teal and grey, the water-serpent RILL coiled around one shoulder and arm, head raised,
    tongue tasting the air. No weapon - and no need of one.
    (Cat and serpent lock eyes. Two bonded souls sizing each other up. Neither moves.
     Two full panels of held tension. Let the art breathe.)
    SEDGE: You're loud. All three of you. The cat especially. I heard you coming since the treeline.
    SEDGE: Fire-people. You smell like ash and you don't know how to walk in water.

p6  ROOK: We are fire-people. Our home burned. We came west with the smoke.
    SEDGE: Everyone's home burns eventually. Why bring your trouble to my water?
    ROOK: Because I didn't know your water was yours. I thought we were the last Vael alive.
    SEDGE (quiet): ...say that again.
    ROOK: The last. I thought my people were the last Vael in the world.
    SEDGE: That's what the Marsh-Mother says. Every winter. "We are the last. Hold together,
           because there is no one else."
    SEDGE: And here you are. Standing in my reeds. Smelling of ash. Not the last.

p7  SORREL: I only bonded three nights ago. This is Moth. Is yours - is Rill a she? Can I -
    (Rill extends its head toward Moth. The cub, half-asleep, boops noses with the serpent.)
    SEDGE: The bond doesn't care what shape the other half takes. Cats, serpents. It just
           wants the halves back together.
    ROOK: Our Keeper says the same. Different words. Same fire.
    SEDGE: ...The Marsh-Mother will want to see you. She won't want to. But she'll want to.
    SEDGE: Keep your hands where Rill can see them. And tell your cat to stop looking at me like dinner.

p8  CAPTION: There was a path. You could only see it if you already knew it was there.
    CAPTION: The reeds opened - and revealed it.

p9  SPLASH. The Marsh Vael village: stilt-huts and floating reed-rafts strung across a wide
    still lagoon, half-hidden in mist. Serpent-bonded Vael pole narrow boats between rafts;
    woven bridges sway; teal lanterns hang. Figures stop and stare.
    CAPTION: The forest Vael had names for many things. They had no name for this.

p10 SORREL (whisper): They're afraid of us.
    ROOK (low): We came out of the fire-direction. To them, we might be the thing that burns.
    MARSH VAEL (scattered): Fire-people... fire-people came... fire-people -
    (MARSH-MOTHER BRACKEN rises. Straight-backed despite her age, teal-and-bone robes, a great
     pale serpent draped at her feet. Her face is a closed door.)
    BRACKEN: Sedge. You brought fire into my reeds.
    SEDGE: I brought Vael, Marsh-Mother. Living Vael. Not the last of us after all.

p11 BRACKEN: Every child in this marsh is raised on one truth. We are the last. We survive
             because we have no one else, and we trust no one else.
    BRACKEN: And now a boy with a cat wants me to unlearn it in an afternoon.
    ROOK: I'm not asking you to unlearn anything. I thought the same, three days ago. Then I found you.
    ROOK: We don't want your water or your rafts. We want to know how far the fire is. And
          whether there are others - sky, stone, wherever the old stories scattered us.
    BRACKEN: The scattering is not a story for guests. It is a wound. And wounds have two sides.
    SEDGE (quiet, to Rook): I don't know what she means. She's never told us either.

p12 SCOUT: Marsh-Mother! The east channel - smoke! Something's cutting the reeds - cutting them straight -
    ROOK: ...straight. Bracken - did you say straight?
    BRACKEN: The reeds don't cut straight. Nothing here cuts straight.
    ROOK: No. Nothing does. It followed us. It's here.

p13 ROOK: How many ways in? Can they burn wet reed?
    SEDGE: Green reed won't take fire fast. But if they cut a straight channel, they drain the
           lagoon - and then it will.
    ROOK: Then we don't let them cut. Sedge - your people know the water. Mine know how to
          fight what comes on land. Together we hold the channel.
    SEDGE: You've known us since breakfast and you want to bleed for our reeds?
    ROOK: I want to not be alone again. That means the reeds are worth bleeding for.
    (Bracken, overhearing, watches Rook - grievance and grudging respect at war.)

p14 The east channel. Mist and smoke. An Ironfolk cutting-crew - angular, soot-grey,
    straight-edged - drives a bladed machine through the reeds, laying a dead-straight scar.
    No bonded creatures. Cold and methodical.
    CAPTION: They did not shout. They did not rage. They simply advanced, and the world went
             straight where they passed.
    SEDGE: On your word, fire-boy.
    ROOK: Now.

p15 SFX: KRRSH - HSSSS
    (Flame-lynxes hit the crew on land; serpents come up out of the water to drag the Ironfolk
     off their machine. Rook and Sedge fight back to back - bone knife and reed-spear.)
    SEDGE: They don't flinch! They don't even look at you - they look through you -
    ROOK: I know. Yarrow warned me. Keep them off the machine!

p16 (The Ironfolk disengage, retreating in a straight line, taking their dead without a word.)
    ROOK: Let them go. We don't know what's behind them. We won.
    SEDGE (breathing hard): That wasn't winning. That was them deciding to stop.
    CAPTION: A thing of straight iron in a world of curves. Deeply wrong.
    ROOK: ...Then we'd better learn everything we can before they decide to start again.

p17 SORREL (laughing): Moth, stop, you can't be jealous of a snake -
    SEDGE: You fought for reeds you saw for the first time this morning.
    ROOK: You pulled me out of a blade you didn't have to.
    SEDGE: ...Rill likes you. Rill has terrible judgment. But she's never wrong.
    ROOK: If there are two of us, Sedge, there are more. Sky. Stone. Wherever the scattering sent us.
    SEDGE: You want to gather them. All of them.
    ROOK: I want to not lose to a machine that cuts straight lines because we were too
          scattered to stand together.
    SEDGE: ...Then you'll need someone who knows the water between here and the mountains.
    ROOK: Are you volunteering?
    SEDGE: I'm saying you'd die without me. Same thing.

p18 YARROW: Your people fight well, Marsh-Mother. So do mine. Odd, how that only mattered
            once we stood on the same side.
    BRACKEN: Don't mistake one fight for trust, forest-crone.
    YARROW: I wouldn't dream of it. Trust takes longer. I only note we're all still alive to argue about it.
    (Bracken's eyes fall to the curved knife at Rook's belt. She goes rigid.)
    BRACKEN (low, to herself): ...that knife.

p19 BRACKEN: Where did you get that blade. The bone one. At your belt.
    ROOK: It was my mother's. And hers before that. Why?
    BRACKEN: Turn it. The heel of the handle. There's a mark cut into the bone.
    ROOK: ...there's a mark. I never knew what it meant.
    BRACKEN: It's the mark of the Marsh Vael. That is our bone. Our making.
    BRACKEN: A forest chief should not be carrying it. Unless a forest chief took it.

p20 ROOK: I don't - my mother gave it to me. I don't know how she -
    BRACKEN: No. You wouldn't. They never tell the children the shameful parts.
    BRACKEN: Long ago the forest Vael and the marsh were one people. And the forest took
             something from us, and left, and let us believe we were alone for it.
    BRACKEN: And now their chief walks in wearing the proof at his belt and asks me to gather.
    (The two tribes pull apart on their rafts. Rook stands alone in the middle, knife in hand.)

p21 SEDGE: Marsh-Mother. He didn't know. You can see he didn't know.
    BRACKEN: What he knew changes nothing about what was done.
    ROOK: I can't answer for what my people did before I was born. I won't pretend I can.
    ROOK: But I'm holding it now. So tell me what was taken. And I'll help you make it right -
          if making it right is possible.
    (A long, terrible silence across the water.)

p22 BRACKEN: You want to make it right.
    BRACKEN: Then I will tell you what was taken. And you will not like where it leads.
    BRACKEN: The forest didn't only take a blade, forest-chief.
    BRACKEN: They took the first of us. The one who remembered the sky before it broke.
    CAPTION: The one who remembered the sky before it broke.
    CAPTION: Next: Episode Three - "Old Wounds."
