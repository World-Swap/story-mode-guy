EMBERWILD — EPISODE ONE: THE BONDING
Full comic script, 22 pages. Supplied by the author.
Tagline: "to bond is to stop being alone."

NOTE ON FORMAT: this script's header specifies "interiors black-and-white with colour
cover" — that is Series Bible v1 and is SUPERSEDED. Handoff notes: "Interiors are FULL
COLOUR. (v1 said black-and-white. That's dead.)" Build in colour.

TONE: warm and earthy for 21 pages, then one cold note at the end. Do not tip the threat early.

p1  SPLASH. Night forest from above, canopy silvered by the enormous broken ring of the
    Riven Sky across the top. Tiny orange campfires far below. Everything curves.
    CAPTION: They say the sky was whole, once.
    CAPTION: They say a great many things. But no one now living remembers it unbroken —
    CAPTION: — and no one remembers what broke it.
    TITLE: EMBERWILD / Episode One — The Bonding

p2  Camp hollow ringed by ancient trees. Hide tents, drying racks, woven lanterns.
    CAPTION: The Vael move when the land tells them to. Tonight, the land said: stay.
    SORREL (small): Tonight.
    ROOK: You haven't eaten.
    SORREL: Can't. Stomach's full of moths.
    ROOK: Everyone's stomach is full of moths, their first bonding. Mine was.
    SORREL: You? You lead the whole tribe.
    ROOK: I threw up on the Keeper's feet.

p3  SORREL: He's so big.
    ROOK: Cinder? He was a runt. Smallest of his litter.
    ROOK: The bond doesn't care about big. It cares whether you're honest. Lynxes can't stand a liar.
    BRAM: Or whether you're worthy. Some people the fire just says no to.
    BRAM: You know it can refuse you? Walk you into the fire circle and just — nothing. No lynx comes.
    BRAM: Happened to a boy two camps ago. He left. Nobody says his name.
    ROOK: The fire hasn't refused a Vael child in my lifetime, Bram. Go frighten someone your own size.

p4  BRAM: Whatever you say. Chief.
    CAPTION: Rook had led for two winters. Since the fever took his mother.
    CAPTION: He did not yet feel like a chief. He felt like a boy wearing his mother's knife.
    SORREL: Is it true? That it can say no?
    ROOK: It's true. But it won't say no to you.
    SORREL: How do you know?
    ROOK: Because you're scared of it. Only the honest ones are scared.

p5  SFX: DHOOM  (great hide drum, concentric rings rippling out)
    YARROW: The land said stay. The fire says now. A child of the Vael is ready to be made whole!
    CAPTION: Every Vael is born half a soul. The other half waits in the fire.

p6  The fire burns strangely — too tall, too orange, shapes moving within.
    YARROW (off): Look into it, child. Not with your eyes.
    SORREL (whisper): I don't — I don't see anything —
    YARROW: You're looking. Stop looking. Ask.
    (Tight on Sorrel's eye, fire reflected — two smaller lights like eyes opening in the flame.)
    SORREL (tiny): ...is someone there?

p7  MONEY SHOT. From the fire, a lynx cub of ember-light steps out, coalescing into fur and
    flesh as it walks toward her. Art Nouveau flame-tendrils curl around it.
    CAPTION: They do not come from the fire. They come through it —
    CAPTION: — from wherever the other half of a Vael soul has been waiting.

p8  The cub — small, silver-and-orange, tiny horn-buds, oversized paws — stops before her.
    SORREL: Hello.
    (The cub presses its forehead into her palm. Soft pulse of light, radiating rings.)
    SFX (soft): fmm
    SORREL: Oh. Oh. I can — I can feel you. You were always —
    CAPTION: To bond is to stop being alone in a way you never knew you were.

p9  TRIBE: HAI! HAI! HAI!
    YARROW: The soul is whole! What name burns in you, child? Speak it once — it cannot be unspoken.
    SORREL: Moth! Her name is Moth — because she's the thing my stomach was full of, and now she's out.

p10 CAPTION: The Vael have few certain things. So they hold hard to the ones they have.
    SORREL: You were right. It didn't say no.
    ROOK: I'm right about one thing a season. You caught me on a good night.
    SORREL: Rook — where do they wait? Before? The Keeper says "the other half of your soul," but where is it?
    ROOK: ...I don't know.
    ROOK: No one does. Same place the sky went, maybe.

p11 BRAM: Good bonding. The girl did well.
    ROOK: She did.
    BRAM: My father led before your mother. You know that.
    ROOK: I know.
    BRAM: I'm not saying it should be me. I'm saying — you don't have to carry it like it's a stone.
          You're allowed to let people in.
    ROOK: That almost sounded like you like me, Bram.
    BRAM: Don't get used to it.

p12 CAPTION: It was, everyone agreed later, one of the good nights.
    (Cinder's eyes snap open in the dark. Ears up.)
    SFX (low): grrrrrrr

p13 ROOK (thought): Cinder. What is it.
    ROOK: ...that's not the dawn.
    (Reveal: far off above the black tree-line east, a dull orange glow. Wrong direction, wrong colour.)

p14 YARROW: I smelled it in the fire tonight. Under the child's joy. Smoke that isn't ours.
    ROOK: How far?
    YARROW: Far. But it's coming. It always comes closer.
    ROOK: What is it, Keeper? What burns like that?
    YARROW: The old word is Ironfolk. My grandmother used it to frighten me.
    YARROW: I did not think I would live to use it to frighten you.

p15 BRAM: The lynxes woke the whole east side. Rook — the glow —
    ROOK: I see it.
    BRAM: Do we run?
    ROOK: We don't run in the dark and lose half the little ones to the ravines.
    ROOK: We break camp at first light. Quiet. Fast. East is fire — so we go west.

p16 CAPTION: The Vael move when the land tells them to.
    CAPTION: This time the land did not ask.
    SORREL: I only just bonded. Is she — is Moth strong enough to travel?
    ROOK: She'll be strong enough. So will you. You have to be now.
    SORREL: Then let's go.

p17 CAPTION: They left the good hollow behind, and the ashes of a bonding fire, still warm.
    CAPTION: Rook walked last. A chief walks last, his mother had told him, so he is the final
             thing between his people and the dark.

p18 (Their POV from a ridge: a black scar cut through the forest, straight as no natural thing is.)
    ROOK: ...the trees. Look how it's cut. Straight. Nothing in the forest moves in a straight line.
    YARROW: The world is curves, boy. Rivers, roots, the sky's own broken ring. Only one thing
            draws a straight line.
    ROOK: What?
    YARROW: Something that wants to.

p19 ROOK: We keep west. We find deep water or high stone — somewhere fire won't follow.
    ELDER: And if west is fire too? If it's everywhere?
    ROOK: Then we'll know more than we know now. Fear's a poor scout. We keep moving.

p20 SORREL: You said only the honest ones are scared.
    ROOK: I did.
    SORREL: You're scared. I can tell.
    ROOK: I'm terrified.
    SORREL: Good. Means you're honest.
    ROOK: ...you're going to be a problem, aren't you.
    SORREL: Probably.

p21 CAPTION: By dusk they had gone farther west than any of them had ever walked.
    CAPTION: Into land the tribe had no names for.
    ROOK: ...alright. New land, new names.
    ROOK: Let's find out what's down there.

p22 ROOK: Cinder. There — by the water. Do you see —
    (Reveal: across the river, half-hidden in the reeds, a Vael with NO flame-lynx — instead a
     long water-serpent coiled around their shoulders.)
    CAPTION: The Vael believed themselves the last of their kind.
    (The stranger's head turns sharply. The serpent rears, hood flaring.)
    CAPTION: They were wrong.
    CAPTION: Next: Episode Two — "Strangers in the Reeds."
