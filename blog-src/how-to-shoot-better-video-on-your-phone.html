Learning how to shoot video on your phone is the fastest way to improve your footage, because the camera in your pocket is far better than most people treat it. Modern phones record sharp 4K with strong stabilization. What holds the footage back isn't the hardware; it's the way it's used. Here's what I tell friends who ask me for phone video tips, and it all comes down to a handful of habits.

Clean the lens and shoot horizontal

It sounds silly, but a fingerprint smudge is the most common reason phone video looks soft and glowy. Wipe the lens with a soft cloth before every shoot. Then turn the phone sideways. Horizontal fills a TV, a laptop, and YouTube. Only shoot vertical when you know the video is going to Reels, TikTok, or Shorts.

Lock focus and exposure

This is the single biggest upgrade for phone videography. By default your phone constantly re-decides what to focus on and how bright to make the image. The result is that pulsing, shifting look where the picture brightens and darkens as you move.

  1. Tap on your subject to set focus.
  2. Press and hold until you see "AE/AF Lock" (on iPhone) or a similar lock on Android.
  3. Slide the little sun icon up or down to adjust brightness. Protect the bright parts of the sky; it's easier to keep detail in the highlights than to rescue them later.

Do this for every new shot and the whole video feels more professional immediately.

Choose your frame rate once

Your phone's camera settings let you pick resolution and frame rate. For most things, 4K at 24 or 30 fps is the sweet spot. Use 24 if you want a more filmic feel, 30 for general use, and 60 only when you intend to slow things down. The important thing is to pick one and stay consistent across a project, so clips cut together cleanly.

A tip specific to phones: because the lens is small and the sensor is tiny, bright days force an aggressively fast shutter, which makes motion look sharp and jittery. Clip-on ND filters designed for phones are cheap and genuinely worth it if you shoot outside a lot.

Steady the shot

Phone stabilization is good but not magic. Walking and holding the phone out in front of you still produces bounce. Try these:

  • Hold the phone with both hands and tuck your elbows into your ribs.
  • Bend your knees slightly and walk heel-to-toe like you're sneaking up on someone.
  • Rest the phone against a wall, railing, car roof, or your own chest for stationary shots.
  • Use a small tripod or a phone gimbal for anything you want to look deliberate.

And think before you move. A steady, still shot almost always beats a restless, wandering one.

Use the light you have

Phones struggle in dim light. Shoot near windows, in open shade, or in the soft light of early morning and just before sunset. In Santa Cruz, that golden hour along West Cliff is hard to beat. Put the light in front of or beside your subject, not behind them, unless you want a silhouette. Avoid digital zoom altogether; if you need a tighter shot, switch to the telephoto lens or simply walk closer.

Fix the audio

Viewers forgive slightly imperfect video far more than bad sound. The built-in mic picks up wind, room echo, and everything else around you. Get the phone close to whoever is talking, or plug in a small lavalier or clip-on wireless mic. Even a basic one makes a huge difference. If you're outdoors, add a furry windscreen.

Shoot variety, then edit tight

A single long take is boring to watch. Capture a mix of shots instead:

  • A wide establishing shot to show where you are.
  • A medium shot of the action or person.
  • Close-ups of hands, faces, and details.

Hold each one for at least ten seconds, even if you'll only use three or four. Then cut every clip as short as you can while it still makes sense. Free editors like CapCut, iMovie, or DaVinci Resolve are plenty to get started.

A quick pre-shoot checklist

  1. Wipe the lens.
  2. Turn on Do Not Disturb, so a notification doesn't interrupt the recording.
  3. Check you have storage space.
  4. Pick your resolution and frame rate.
  5. Lock focus and exposure on each shot.
