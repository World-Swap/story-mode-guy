I've spent more evenings than I can count standing on the Santa Cruz coast waiting for the light to do its thing. Some of those evenings were a bust. Most weren't. Over the years I've settled into a short list of spots I keep coming back to — each one good for a slightly different kind of sunset. Here's where I go, when I go there, and what to expect when you show up with a camera (or just a folding chair).

1. West Cliff Drive

If you only have time for one spot, make it West Cliff. The paved path runs right along the bluffs, so you get an unbroken western horizon and the surf breaking below you. Park anywhere between Lighthouse Point and Natural Bridges and walk until you find a stretch of railing you like.

Best for: big open-sky sunsets, silhouettes of surfers, and that classic "rose clouds over the bluff" look. Timing: arrive 30 minutes before the listed sunset time — the best color often comes after the sun is already down.

2. Natural Bridges State Beach

The remaining sea arch here gives you a foreground that almost no other spot in town can match. When the sun lines up behind the bridge in late fall and winter, it's genuinely one of the best compositions on the whole California coast.

Best for: a strong foreground anchor. Heads up: the state park lot closes at sunset, so park on the street outside the gate if you plan to stay for the afterglow.

3. The Santa Cruz Wharf

Shoot the wharf, or shoot from it. From the beach you get the pilings marching out to sea with the sun sinking behind them. From the end of the wharf you're out over the water with the whole bay opening up in front of you.

Best for: pilings-and-silhouette shots, and long-lens compression of the sun dropping into the water.

4. The Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk

The boardwalk is the one that surprises people. Wait for the ten-minute window right after the sky goes dark — the blue hour — when the sky is still deep blue but the ride lights are already on. The Giant Dipper and the Ferris wheel against that color is the shot.

Best for: blue-hour and night shots with color and motion. Timing: this happens roughly 15–25 minutes after sunset, later than most people wait around for.

5. Lighthouse Point & Steamer Lane

A little headland with a lot going on — the lighthouse, the surf break, and a clean view down the coast. Great when there's weather offshore and the clouds are catching fire.

6. Its Beach (below the lighthouse)

Down the stairs from Lighthouse Point, this small beach gives you wet sand for reflections and the bluffs framing the light. Go at low tide for the mirror effect on the flats.

7. The Santa Cruz Mountains, looking back

If you want something different, drive up into the hills for a sunset over the bay instead of on it. You trade the surf foreground for elevation and layers of ridgeline. Redwoods and fog make it feel like a different world ten minutes from downtown.

A few things I've learned

  • Stay for the afterglow. The best color usually comes after the sun is gone. Most people leave too early.
  • Check the clouds, not just the clock. A totally clear sky gives you a clean gradient but no drama. High or mid-level clouds are what catch fire.
  • Face away from the sun too. While everyone points west, the eastern sky often glows a soft pink — the "Belt of Venus." Some of my favorite shots are the ones behind me.
  • Low tide changes everything. Wet sand and tide pools give you reflections and foreground you don't get at high water.
