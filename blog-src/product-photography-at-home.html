If you sell anything online, your photos are your storefront. The good news is that product photography at home doesn't require a studio, strobes, or a big budget. With a window, about $30 of supplies, and a few settings, you can shoot clean images that look like they came from a pro. I've set this up on plenty of kitchen tables, including my own, and it works every time.

The $30 shopping list

Here's everything you need, and none of it is fancy:

  • Two sheets of white foam board or poster board (about $5 to $8). One is your background and sweep; the other is your reflector.
  • A roll of white or black paper, or a few colored poster boards (about $8) for backdrops.
  • A cheap phone tripod (about $12). This is the single best upgrade you can make, because it keeps every image consistent.
  • Painter's tape to hold everything in place.

Any camera works, including your phone. Consistency matters more than megapixels.

Build the setup around a window

Natural window light is soft, free, and flattering. Place a table next to a large window, ideally one that doesn't get direct sun. A north-facing window, or a window on a gray day, is perfect. Direct sun creates hard, contrasty shadows that make products look cheap.

  1. Put the table sideways to the window, so the light hits your product from the side.
  2. Curve one white board from the table up the wall behind the product. That seamless sweep gives you a clean background with no hard edge.
  3. Place the second white board on the shadow side of the product. It bounces light back and opens up the dark side.
  4. Set the camera on the tripod at a height that matches how customers will view the item.

Control the shadows

Shadow is what makes a product look three-dimensional, so don't remove it entirely. Move the reflector closer to lighten shadows and farther away to deepen them. Try black card on the shadow side if the item is glossy and needs defined edges. For items on reflective surfaces, like jewelry or glass, shoot at a slight angle so you don't capture your own reflection.

The settings I'd use

On a camera, start with these values and adjust from there:

  • Aperture: f/8 to f/11 so the entire product is sharp front to back.
  • ISO: 100 to 200 for the cleanest image. Window light plus a tripod means you can afford a slow shutter.
  • Shutter speed: whatever gives a correct exposure, often 1/15 to 1/2 second. Use a two-second timer so touching the shutter doesn't blur anything.
  • White balance: set it manually or use a gray card. Don't leave it on auto if you're shooting a whole catalog.

On a phone, tap the product to lock focus, drag the exposure slider down until whites aren't clipping, and avoid digital zoom. Walk the tripod closer instead.

Shots every listing needs

Etsy and other marketplaces reward buyers who can see the item clearly. For most products, I shoot:

  1. A clean main image on a plain background, the whole item centered with some breathing room.
  2. A back or side angle.
  3. A close-up of texture, stitching, or detail.
  4. A scale shot with a hand or a familiar object.
  5. A lifestyle shot, like a mug on a wooden table with a window behind it.

Edit for consistency

Once you've shot the set, edit every photo the same way. Crop to the same ratio, usually square or 4:5. Brighten the background until it reads clean white without blowing out the product edges. Fix color so the product matches what a customer will actually receive, since returns come from photos that lie. Free tools like Snapseed, Lightroom Mobile, or your phone's editor are plenty. Save a preset or note the settings so the next batch matches.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing window light with room lights. Turn the lamps off, because the color clashes.
  • Dust, fingerprints, and wrinkles. Wipe and steam everything before you shoot.
  • Changing the background and angle from one product to the next.
  • Over-editing until colors look unreal.
